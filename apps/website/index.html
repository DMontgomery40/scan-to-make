How it works

From the real thing to a part that fits.

  1. 01

    Scan it

    Walk around the object with your iPhone. The app guides you round every side.

  2. 02

    See how close it is

    Your companion cleans up the scan, measures it, and compares it with the real size, side by side, in millimetres.

  3. 03

    Add close-ups

    A scan gets the shape right but blurs small things. Photos of ports, buttons and edges keep those details.

  4. 04

    Make and print

    Pick what to make, check it can print, then send it to your printer. You stay in charge of every print.

Checks, not guesses

You see the numbers before you print.

A scan is a good start, not a promise. Scan to Make shows you how close it is, and checks the print before anything heats up.

  • Measured against the real size

    Use the maker’s published size for a known product, or your own measurements. Every side gets a plain verdict.

  • Printability checked first

    Does it fit the bed, sit flat, and have walls the nozzle can print? If it needs supports, you’re asked before they’re added.

  • You confirm, your companion double-checks

    You check the plate and the filament. Your companion checks the printer again before it starts, and never retries a print on its own.

  • Honest about fit

    A finished print still needs checking against the real thing. We’ll always say so.

Your printer, your computer

The heavy lifting happens on your companion.

Your iPhone scans and you make the choices. A companion computer you own cleans up the scan, slices the print and talks to your printer, over your own private network.

With your own companion, your scans and photos stay on your devices.

Questions

Good to know.

Do I need a 3D printer?

Yes. Parts print on your own printer. Bambu Lab printers come first, and more printers will follow.

Which iPhones can scan?

Scanning uses Apple’s Object Capture, which needs an iPhone with a LiDAR Scanner. You can also bring in a 3D scan you already have.

What is the companion?

It’s software that runs on a computer you own. Today that’s a Mac. It does the scan clean-up, the slicing, and the careful hand-off to your printer.

Is it open source?

Scan to Make is being built in the open so the core can be self-hosted. Licence details will be published before release.

How much will it cost?

We’ll share pricing before launch.

# Operating Counterpart

How to change, gate, build, install and drive Counterpart. What is built and accepted is in the [working PRD](design/overhaul/PRD.md); who owns what and what's installed is in [`status/handoff.md`](status/handoff.md).

## David's rules

- **Acceptance is David pressing real buttons in the real installed app** (Mac or iPhone) driving the real printer, judged by the printer's own reports. Unit tests, scripts, direct MCP calls, the control socket, ssh and Studio/Handy are engineering checks only. Never stop, pause or resume the printer outside Counterpart's UI to make a test pass. Never restart FULU/Colima or repair state to manufacture a result. Use the published MCP packages users receive through Download/Update; hand-copied builds don't count as release acceptance.
- **No autonomous prints or reprints.** David, Nov 4: "do not reprint anything on your own EVER." David starts every physical print. "Plate clear" is only a status.
- **The printer MCPs are the backend.** A button is one MCP tool call. A bug in an MCP is fixed once, in the MCP, and in both MCPs (bambu-printer-mcp and mcp-3D-printer-server) when it applies to both. No parallel backends or copies of MCP state. Download/Update follows npm latest, independently of app releases.
- **Don't invent rules or "improvements".** Change what was asked; when he has decided, don't re-ask. (Removing the FULU control's 5-second hold made him furious; it stays.)
- **Just works:** auto-detect, dropdowns, smart-filled `.env`; advanced users can still edit files.
- **Watch the app yourself** (accessibility dump, `events.ndjson`, `object.json`, `control-trace.log`); never make him screenshot it. Don't end a turn while something you started is in flight.
- **Device Hub, mirroring and devicectl are allowed.** David: "i didn't mean never use device hub again, i meant be careful and aware of it's issues borking the camera". A connected Continuity display blacks out camera/LiDAR frames: [`status/camera-incident-2026-11-02.md`](status/camera-incident-2026-11-02.md).
- **Before replacing the Mac app or restarting the companion,** check through the app that nothing is printing or working, and keep a rollback.
- **Never take David's screen.** David, Nov 6: "use t3 connect not ssh, why am i seeing teminal pop up and stealing my cursor". No Terminal windows, `.command` files, `osascript ... activate`, or anything that brings an app forward; open apps with `open -g`. Never press a Counterpart control that opens a window or sheet (Print, Print Again…, Settings, Update, Move…) for checks or screenshots: read state through AX, the companion socket and project files, and capture only windows already on screen. Mac work that needs the Mac's own permissions runs in the Mac's T3 environment or as a headless gui/501 LaunchAgent.
- **Printing beats gating.** This is about the moment a print starts, and about tests not failing under load. Once a print has started, nobody watches it and it delays nothing, installs included (David, Nov 10: "you don't have to monitor prints once they start ... if a print starts, it's fine, and if it's not, it's a slicer issue, and we're not a slicer"). The Mac has one build lane with two slots ([`../scripts/README.md`](../scripts/README.md), "The Mac's build lane"): every `xcodebuild` (builds and test runs, not only gates) and every simulator from boot to shutdown runs inside a slot, as `python3 scripts/lane.py --label "<who>: <what>" -- <command>`. Two normal holds can run at once. `--exclusive` takes both slots, for what must not share the machine: the gate's iPhone/iPad UI test job (`ci_local.py` does this itself; UI tests go flaky under contention and a false failure costs 22 minutes) and anything that starts a print. Callers queue in arrival order, and normal callers that arrive after a waiting exclusive one wait behind it. `python3 scripts/lane.py --status` shows who holds each slot, for how long, and who is waiting. A hold takes 10 minutes at most, except the gate's UI job; `--max-minutes` declares a longer one, and `--status` reports a hold past it as overdue (nobody kills it). Never leave a simulator booted outside a slot. Queue a hold as a background command so its exit wakes you, and never end a turn while it is queued or running. (Nov 6: parallel agent gates pushed the Mac to load 300 and broke a print start. Nov 8: agents building and keeping simulators booted outside the lock pushed it to about 800 during a print.) **One simulator session at a time** (corrected Nov 10 after an hour of two: load average 690, 0.3% CPU idle, 211 MB of memory free, and David's installed app took 121 s to answer a slicing estimate that takes 5): every hold that boots a simulator passes `--exclusive`; only holds that just compile share the two slots.
- **Never ask the person what is published.** David, Nov 11, when a holder for his 2016 Forester came back asking him for "a photo of the dash spot": "you fucking have every single square centimenter of the inside of a forester mapped, you don't have to guess on material ... every fucking curve and surace and ideal spot based on every factor ... don't ever ask the user a question so fucking insanely online again, what the fuck do you think the point of this app is". A named car, phone, case, tool or standard part has its dimensions, materials, drawings, fitted-accessory positions and owner measurements published. The design pass (the app's worker, a kernel lab, a delegate, the lead) finds them, cites them, and designs to them. "Not found" is written only with the list of places searched, and it is a reason to search harder or design around the gap, not a question for him. A question goes to the person only for what exists nowhere but in his hands (how something felt, which of his own objects, a choice of his).
- **Every real use of the product goes through the app, as a user.** David, Nov 11, when the lead created a project and queued a print through the companion's control socket: "YOU MUST DO IT THOUGH THE APP AS A USER"; "DO NOTHING THAT A USER COULD NOT DO INCLUDING THE LINK, PASTE THE LINK IN, WHAT THE FUCK DO YOU THINK YOU'RE TESTING?"; "WHY THE FUCK YOU WOULD SCRIPT A PRINT, WHILE BUILDING AND TESTING AN APP TO PRINT, THEREBY MAKING YOUR DATA COLLECTION OPPORTUNITY FUCKING USELESS". The lead and the delegates are the app's only users besides him, so each time one of them needs a project made, a link or file added, a change asked for, a question answered or a print started for real, it is done in an app's own interface, pressing what a person presses: the Mac app through the Codex computer-use MCP (any model can call it; never bring a window forward or take his cursor), or the phone app on a simulator paired to his Mac the way a phone pairs, driven with `agent-device`. Nothing a user could not do: no control-socket call that creates, changes, answers or prints; no file placed by path; no parameter set that the screen does not offer. The control socket is for reading state. Everything met on the way (a refusal, a missing path, a control that cannot be found, a wait with no words, a wrong credit, a title cut off) is a board row in the same turn, in the words a person would use, with a screenshot. If the app cannot be driven (the computer-use helper will not start, no simulator is free), the action waits and the lead says so; it is not scripted instead.
- **Core actions need end-to-end tests.** A change to Print, Update, Move, plate selection, Add printer or Send ships with a test that presses the control and checks the companion call and the visible result, on every layout it touches. After every Mac install, check read-only that Settings → Printers lists every MCP within seconds and a real `objects.estimate` answers.
- **Read Bambu Studio's source, don't guess** (`~/refs/BambuStudio`, v02.08.02.61, on the Mac and agents-136). Cite file:line for 3MF, CLI, G-code or AMS behaviour. Studio is AGPL-3.0 and the MCPs GPL-2.0: reimplement behaviour, never paste code.
- No user or agent memories (AGENTS.md). Push after every feature. Clean up the worktrees, branches, caches and containers you create. iOS/macOS 27, Liquid Glass; read current Apple docs and use Apple's frameworks.

## Looking at the app

David, Nov 9, after a question's highlighted area showed in a 64-point thumbnail and "What you sent" showed 77 near-identical scan photos: "is there no reviewing thing that actually looks at what was just made and says 'huh... is this logical and human readable and intuitive and visible and the best ux experience possible?'" Reviews read diffs and UI tests use one-photo fixtures, so look at the screens filled with real projects before a UI change is called done.

- `python3 scripts/ux_screens.py <out> --work <scratch>` on the Mac makes screenshots of every iPhone/iPad screen on the iPhone, iPad, and iPhone Duo folded and unfolded, and writes `<out>/index.md` (screen, state, device, and whether the data is real or fixture). Names are stable, so two runs compare side by side. `--devices iphone` and `--screens home,project-wall` narrow a run; `--project <id>` captures any copied project's three tabs.
- Real data, never the real app: it clones the projects (not `auth`, `tls`, `companion.env`, printer handoff or profiles) into `<scratch>`, rewrites paths into the copy, and serves them with the real companion on a free port of its own, with an empty environment and a printer MCP of `/usr/bin/false`. The simulator reaches it through a proxy on the first free simulator test address (`http://127.0.0.1` on 9078, or 9080–9097 in Debug builds), so several prepared runs can wait for the lane at once. Screens that need a printer or a running pass use the app's fixtures and are labelled so.
- It runs itself through `scripts/lane.py --exclusive` (label `ux-screens: <devices>`) and stops starting screens at `--max-minutes` (default 10). Images are kept as they are taken; run the same command again to go on. A full layout takes three or four holds.
- It builds the app and the companion from the checkout whenever their source differs from what they were last built from, and prints and records (per image, in `index.md`) the commit. Each kind of device has one kept simulator (`stm-ux-iphone`, `stm-ux-ipad`, `stm-ux-duo`): created once, the app reinstalled and the keychain reset between runs, shut down at the end of every hold. `--fresh-simulator` creates and deletes one instead (the old way). Every hold logs the Mac's load average and its time per screen to `<out>/runs.jsonl`.
- **A bad day** (David, Nov 10, after "What you scanned" stayed blank on his phone while it fetched a large scan: "you are STILL ALLLOWING BAD UX TO MAKE IT THROUGH THE GATE"). `--condition` makes the capture companion's proxy behave like a real Mac on a bad day; the app and the companion run unchanged:
  - `slow`: every reply waits 1.5 s, and files arrive at about 1 MB/s (a 16 MB scan takes 16 s);
  - `refusing`: file downloads answer 413 (models and scans) or 404 (pictures), while the JSON API works;
  - `away`: the project list loads once, then nothing answers.

  Use one output folder per condition (the tool refuses to mix them), for example `--condition slow <out>-slow`.
- **The blank check.** At the end of every capture, `scripts/ux_blank.py` reads each frame with its accessibility tree (`<frame>.ax.json`) and flags three kinds of frame:
  - a picture stage (known by its controls: the size chip, "N photos", "AR", "Explore in 3D") with nothing drawn above them and no progress or words;
  - an unlabelled picture of 64 pt or more that is one flat colour;
  - a frame where the app was not on screen.

  For main's stage (`gallery-stage-picture`, #539) it reads the state from the element's value and the drawing from the pixels: "shown" must draw something, "loading" and "not available" must draw their line of words (a bare backdrop, or a spinner alone, fails). It cannot tell a picture of something else. It writes `<out>/blank.md` and the run exits non-zero when anything is flagged, so a gate can run `python3 scripts/ux_screens.py <out>-slow --condition slow --devices iphone --screens <the changed screens>` and fail on a blank. `python3 scripts/ux_blank.py <out>` re-checks saved frames without a simulator.
- The companion is built from the checkout when needed (`npm ci` then `npm run build`), and needs Node. The scratch folder holds the person's photos: keep it out of Git and move it to `/Volumes/Spillover` afterwards.
- Not covered yet: the Mac app (its design mode opens windows on David's screen; it needs an off-screen renderer), Android and Linux, the Lock Screen.

## Changing code: one trunk

- `main` is the only trunk. Feature branches never ship, and none lives long: finished work lands on main the day it is finished, on a train.
- **The train** (David, Nov 9, after eight finished branches sat unmerged for days: "squash all the changes together that are done so far into one branch and then run the review over that branch of everything that's ready"). One gate and one review cover everything that is ready, instead of each branch gating alone and sending the others back to rebase.
  - A delegate builds on its own branch, runs only the focused tests for what it touched, pushes, opens a PR and reports. It does not run the full gate or `branch_check`, and never force-pushes once its PR is open.
  - The lead (or a conductor delegate) merges every ready branch into `train/<yyyy-mm-dd>` with merge commits (`git merge --no-ff`, so each PR's commits and credit survive and GitHub marks it merged), resolves conflicts keeping both sides, and opens one PR for the train.
  - One review of the combined diff (Muse Wonder Full), conflict resolutions first, and one full gate on the train. Fixes go on the train as small commits; only the jobs a fix touches are rerun.
  - **Waiting, failed and empty are screens too.** David, Nov 10, an hour into a build whose new gallery showed nothing where the scan should be: "you are STILL ALLLOWING BAD UX TO MAKE IT THROUGH THE GATE". Every test and capture had talked to a companion on the same machine, which answers at once and never refuses; his phone waits on Wi-Fi for files of 2 to 192 MB from a busy Mac. A PR that adds anything a screen has to wait for (a download, a model, a picture, an answer from the Mac or an MCP) shows three more frames, or it does not join a train: while it waits, after it is refused or fails, and when there is nothing. None of them may be an empty stage or card: each shows what is already known (the Mac's picture, the last value) and one plain line. The screenshot tool's conditions (`--condition slow|refusing|away`) and its blank check run on every train before it merges: `python3 scripts/ux_gate.py ~/scan-to-make-work/gate-<train>` on the Mac, with the train's branch checked out, captures every project page on the iPhone and the iPad under all three, in several exclusive lane holds, and exits non-zero on any blank frame (its `gate.md` lists them). Slow is David's everyday link (board X100).
  - **Someone looks at it.** David, Nov 9, after a question's highlighted area shipped as a 64-point thumbnail and a project page as a wall of 77 near-identical photos: "is there no reviewing thing that actually looks at what was just made and says 'huh... is this logical and human readable and intuitive and visible and the best ux experience possible?'" There was not: reviews read diffs, the gate runs tests, and UI-test fixtures hold one photo and a short sentence. A train that changes anything a person sees does not land until its changed screens have been captured filled with real project data, on iPhone, iPad and the Duo folded and unfolded (and the Mac where it can be rendered without taking David's screen), and looked at against four questions: can the important thing be seen at arm's length; is anything repeated or boilerplate; is the next action obvious; would it embarrass us beside an app Apple features. The before and after pictures go in the train's PR. Who looks (David, Nov 11: "We don't need a full council run on every single thing"): the lead, at the captures, for a routine train; the three-member council in AGENTS.md only for a redesign of a screen, or when the lead's look leaves a real doubt. A delegate's own PR shows its screens the same way.
  - The train merges to main with a merge commit, and that main is installed on David's Mac and iPhone the same day. Retarget stacked PRs to main first so they close with it.
  - Work that is not ready rides the next train. A branch that misses two trains is finished or archived (`archive/<date>/<name>` tag), not left open.
  - A change with nothing else in flight is a train of one: same gate, same review.
- **Gate:** `CI_LOCAL_LINUX_HOSTS=agents-171 /usr/local/bin/python3 scripts/ci_local.py all --changed-only --base origin/main` on the Mac (Ubuntu jobs go to agents-171; never agents-173). Hosted Actions have no runner. Then `python3 scripts/branch_check.py --merge <branch>` must print SAFE (read it, don't chain it: on Nov 11 the lead chained `gh pr merge` after it and merged #527 past a NOT SAFE (a low-confidence boundary flag, read afterwards and found harmless; the next one may not be)). It includes the Jev boundary check (PRD §8.0). Use one gate at a time per checkout. Before handing a branch to a train, run its UI tests on the gate's own devices (the iPhone the gate picks and an iPad Air 11-inch in landscape): on Nov 10 a search test passed on its author's 13-inch iPad and failed in the gate, where the smaller screen and another branch's taller rows put the result under a bar.
- **Builds refuse non-main or dirty source** (`scripts/trunk_guard.sh`; the list is in [`../scripts/README.md`](../scripts/README.md)). `STM_TRUNK_OVERRIDE="<why>"` is logged and for local debugging only; never install that build for David.
- **No AI attribution.** Commits carry no model `Co-Authored-By` line and PR bodies no generated-with line, here and in both MCP repos (David, Nov 10: "you have 4 labs of models working in the team, just drop attribution, old lead never should have put that"). A person's authorship and co-author credit on existing commits is kept when work is merged or cherry-picked. Both MCP repos have their own review conventions; read each AGENTS.md. In the MCP repos merging publishes to npm.
- `~/scan-to-make` on each machine tracks main and fast-forwards every 2 minutes. `stm-ci <ref>`, run in agents-136's T3 environment, tests a pushed commit on Linux.

## Building and installing

- **Mac:** from a clean main checkout, `bash apps/mac/scripts/prepare-payload.sh` (it archives HEAD; a Release build refuses a payload from another commit), then
  `xcodebuild -project apps/mac/ScanToMake.xcodeproj -scheme ScanToMake -configuration Release -destination 'generic/platform=macOS' -derivedDataPath <out> CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION=<yyyymmddhhmm> STM_ENTITLEMENTS_SUFFIX=+CloudKit -allowProvisioningUpdates build` in a lane slot (`python3 scripts/lane.py --label "<who>: Mac build" -- xcodebuild …`). Over ssh, codesign fails (`errSecInternalComponent`): run the build as a one-off LaunchAgent in `gui/$(id -u)`. Grep the built payload for your change. Install: check the app is idle, close open sheets, quit, back up the old app, `ditto` to `/Applications`, `launchctl kickstart -k gui/$(id -u)/com.scantomake.mac.companion`, open, and log it in a receipt folder. Developer ID releases: `apps/mac/scripts/release.sh` ([`distribution.md`](distribution.md)).
- **iPhone:** `apps/mobile/scripts/install-iphone.sh` (Release, development signing, USB install without uninstalling; receipts outside the checkout). TestFlight: `apps/mobile/scripts/prepare-testflight.sh`.
- **Printer MCPs:** publish the reviewed package, then use Counterpart's own Download/Update while idle and verify the app runs that version. Never hand-copy `dist`.
- **Mac disk during gates:** each ci_local run leaves a 0.7–1.8 GB `stm-ci-local-*` folder in `$TMPDIR`, and Time Machine's local snapshots keep deleted files until thinned. The LaunchAgent `com.scantomake.gate-temp-cleanup` (`~/scan-to-make-work/maintenance/gate-temp-cleanup.sh`, every 30 min) deletes idle gate folders older than 90 min and thins snapshots under 20 GiB free. By hand: `tmutil thinlocalsnapshots / 40000000000 4`.
- **Site:** a Cloudflare Worker; it does not deploy on merge. From a clean main checkout on the Mac (it has the wrangler login): `npx wrangler@4 deploy --config site/wrangler.jsonc`, then check the live URL.
- **Linux:** `apps/linux/scripts/bundle.sh` on Ubuntu 26.04 x86_64. **Android:** `./gradlew assembleDebug` in `android/` (agents-173 has the SDK).

## Driving the apps

- **Mac:** read the app through accessibility (AX) without activating it; a Settings window may be window 1. Don't press controls or send keys in David's app (see David's rules); acceptance presses are David's. Many SwiftUI buttons have no accessible name: find them by size.
- **David's phone is always on his LAN, and often not on his Wi-Fi.** David, Nov 10: "i am hardwired through wireguard in my firewalla router, no matter where i am in the world, on whatever connection, my phone is on my lan". The app reaches the Mac from anywhere, over whatever link is under the tunnel (his Nov 10 screenshots show 5G), so no screen may assume a fast link. `devicectl` and installs are different: they find the phone by local discovery, which does not cross the tunnel, so they need the phone on the house Wi-Fi itself or on a cable. When `xcrun devicectl list devices` says `unavailable`, say that; do not guess that the phone is asleep or locked.
- **Phone:** `xcrun devicectl device capture screenshot --device 00008150-001A488C3642401C --destination <png>`; `devicectl device process launch` to launch.
- **Files:** events `~/Library/Application Support/Scan to Make/events.ndjson`; projects `…/capture/objects/<id>/object.json`; printers `…/printers/profiles.json`; FULU control trace `~/.config/bambu-printer-mcp/bambu-network/control-trace.log`; companion control socket `…/auth/control.sock` (read-only diagnosis; never stand in for the app).
- **zsh on the Mac:** don't `echo =====`; quote globs like `--include='*.ts'`.
- **Second opinions:** a `delegate_task` review (see "Agents, models and machines"), not a CLI on the Mac.

Evidence and receipts live in `~/scan-to-make-work/` on the Mac.
